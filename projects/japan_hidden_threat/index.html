A Hidden Threat?: Investigating the Effects of Radiation on Life Expectancy in Japan

Public Health · Northeastern University · July 2024 · Python

Abstract

The effects of radiation on the health of a population have been studied meticulously since the emergence of nuclear technology in the past century and caused public concern. This study aims to analyze whether nuclear radiation has a strong effect on life expectancy in order to understand the long-lasting implications of radiation exposure. To do so, radiation levels and life expectancy statistics from 2010-2020 from varying prefectures in Japan will be dissected. I use data from national records and environmental databases to run a regression analysis on correlation of the variables and uncover their relationship. My findings showed that radiation did not have a significant effect on life expectancy, so I turn to exploring why that might have been the case, specifically in Fukushima prefecture, as it experienced the nuclear meltdown at Fukushima Dai-ichi nuclear power plant in 2011. Certain factors, such as evacuation, medical care, and community ties have been found to be important in the case of keeping residents in Fukushima safe. Overall, the study helps to gain perspective on the lasting effects of radiation and what actions may be able to curb its harmfulness.
